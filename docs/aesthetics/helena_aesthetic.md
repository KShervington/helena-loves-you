I would describe the aesthetic as uncanny clinical retrofuturism or sterile liminal sci-fi.

It's a vision of the future where everything is immaculate, geometric, and deliberately controlled, but something feels subtly wrong.

The defining characteristics are:

- Hyperclean minimalism: Seamless surfaces, pristine white interiors, almost no clutter.
- Clinical futurism: Architecture and clothing reminiscent of hospitals, laboratories, or spacecraft.
- Controlled color: Mostly white and cool gray, interrupted by isolated, saturated colors—particularly red.
- Uncanny atmosphere: Environments that seem designed for humans but somehow lack humanity.
- Emotional restraint: Stillness, neutral expressions, symmetry, and an almost artificial sense of calm.

The unsettling quality comes from the absence of obvious danger. Everything looks too perfect.

Immaculate white testing chambers, artificial lighting, and an unsettling sense of isolation despite the game's humor.

Ex Machina (2014)

A more contemporary interpretation, with immaculate research spaces, glass, controlled lighting, and emotionally detached human subjects.

There isn't one universally established name for this exact combination, but these search terms should produce useful references:

One distinction I'd preserve is that this aesthetic isn't quite liminal horror. Traditional liminal imagery often relies on abandonment, decay, or empty transitional spaces. What you're describing is more interesting in one respect: the environment is still functioning, someone is present, and everything appears normal.

It's the perfection itself that makes the scene unsettling.
